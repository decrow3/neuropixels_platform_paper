**Figure 5. Large-scale OpenScope recordings provide a within-area benchmark for functional heterogeneity.**

**A)** Cortical cartoon (left) and anatomical-hierarchy schematic (right) for V1 and the five higher visual areas (HVAs) studied in Siegle et al. (2021). Node heights show published anatomical hierarchy scores from Harris et al. (2019). Curved links indicate directed anatomical projections, with ascending projections on the left and descending projections on the right; colors identify the source area, and widths are proportional to normalized anatomical connection density.

**B)** Four spatially separated V1 sampling regions in the OpenScope electrophysiology dataset provide a within-area benchmark for variation among HVA means. The cartoon contrasts sampling within V1 (intra-V1) with sampling across HVAs (inter-HVA; A). Cartoon boundaries and marker positions are illustrative; actual sampling and data-collection methods are described in Figure 4. The schematic at right illustrates group means (dashed lines), ±1 SD of category means (black bars), and a difference between group means (orange arrow). Marker heights are illustrative, not measured responses; alignment with A does not equate functional and anatomical scores.

**C–D)** Single-neuron time to first spike (TTFS), phase modulation (log10 F1/F0), and response timescale in five HVAs (LM, RL, AL, PM and AM) from the Allen Brain Observatory Visual Coding–Neuropixels dataset (C), and four V1 sampling locations from the OpenScope electrophysiology dataset (D). Dots show subsamples of eligible neurons; half-violins show their distributions. Colored diamonds indicate session-adjusted category means from weighted models that balance categories, animals and recordings. Within each population and metric, a common translation centers these fitted means on the equally weighted raw category means while preserving all fitted differences. Adjacent pooled plots combine eligible neurons with equal cell weights and repeat the fitted-mean diamonds. Bars labelled “Cells SD” and “Means SD” span ±1 SD around the pooled cellular mean and the equally weighted fitted-category mean, respectively; these bars describe spread, not confidence intervals. Ant., anterior; Lat., lateral; Post., posterior; Med., medial.

**E)** Point estimates and 95% percentile intervals from 5,000 whole-animal bootstrap replicates. From left to right: the mean difference between equal-area pooled HVAs and V1 in matched Allen sessions (orange); the SD of V1-location means (open circle) and HVA means (open square); and their signed difference, inter-HVA SD minus intra-V1 SD (filled dark circle). The mean comparison uses Allen V1, not the OpenScope V1 population in D. All three sections share the same response-unit y-scale within each metric. Intervals for spread differences that include zero do not establish equivalence or absence of within-V1 or between-HVA differences.

**F)** Location-associated variance, expressed as bias-corrected one-way omega-squared (ω², %), for intra-V1 recording location and inter-HVA area identity, followed by their signed inter-HVA minus intra-V1 difference (blue diamonds). The difference is expressed in percentage points and its 95% interval is calculated from independent-population whole-animal bootstrap differences. No shuffle-test significance symbol is assigned to this contrast. All three contrast intervals include zero; this does not establish equivalence. Points and whiskers show estimates and individual 95% percentile intervals from 5,000 whole-animal bootstrap replicates (not simultaneous intervals). Unlike E’s balanced, session-adjusted category-mean SDs, F uses raw neuronal responses with equal cell weights and no session adjustment; negative estimates are retained. Significance annotations use matching within-session location-block shuffles (5,000 permutations; one-sided tests with Bonferroni correction across the six component tests: three metrics × two populations): * p < .05, ** p < .01, *** p < .001; n.s., p ≥ .05. All neurons in a session/location move together; tests assume exchangeability of available labels within sessions. For MouseV2 timescale, the observed and shuffled ω² statistics average ten draw-specific values, using one coherent label permutation per session across draws. Annotations use adjusted p-values, calculated as min(1, 6 × raw p). Adjusted inter-HVA p-values are 0.002400 for TTFS and 0.001200 for timescale; all remaining component tests have adjusted p ≥ .05. These tests concern each location effect, not the difference between intra-V1 and inter-HVA effects; nonsignificance does not establish absence of an effect. Summary shuffle statistics and exact p-values are supplied in Figure5_F_estimates_and_tests.csv.

Timescale estimation uses 75 bright and 75 dark flashes per dataset. C/D display one fixed MouseV2 trial subsample; E’s timescale category SDs use the square root of the mean category variance across ten subsamples. Bootstrap resampling selects one coherent trial subsample per sampled MouseV2 session occurrence. Full eligibility criteria, sample sizes and supporting analyses are provided in the Methods.
