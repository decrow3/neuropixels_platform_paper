**Figure 4: Within-V1 functional heterogeneity provides a benchmark for differences among mouse higher visual areas.** A) Dorsal Allen CCFv3 surface projection of all 32 MouseV2 V1 probe entry points from eight animals, including the corrected probe assignments for subject 815152. VISp (V1) is shaded, and its border and the surrounding higher-visual-area borders come directly from the Allen `top.nrrd` surface atlas. Each point is the most superficial AP electrode on that probe whose refreshed NWB atlas label starts with VISp; its NWB CCF `(x, z)` coordinates are displayed as CCF `(AP, ML)`. Of the displayed entry proxies, 30/32 fall within the independently defined VISp surface polygon; the two border cases from subject 810532 fall in VISa and VISpm and are retained without adjustment. Colors identify four categorical V1 sampling locations: anterior, lateral, posterior, and medial, shown consistently in that order using red, orange, green, and blue. These reader-facing spatial names correspond internally to probes A, E, C, and B, respectively. B) OpenScope imaging schematic over a widefield retinotopic map. The twelve imaging locations are shown in three equally represented colors (four locations per color) that are spatially interleaved for visualization and do not define three anatomical groups. C) MouseV2 session-level time to first spike (TTFS), log10 F1/F0, and response-timescale estimates at the four V1 sampling locations. MouseV2 neurons are restricted to units whose extremum AP channel is labeled VISp in the refreshed NWB electrodes table; session-by-location cells with fewer than five eligible neurons are omitted. Points are recording-session means, faint half-violins show their distributions, and colored bars and whiskers show the mean and normal-approximation 95% confidence interval. V1 locations are treated as categorical sampling locations, not as positions on a hierarchy axis; they are displayed left to right (and, in F, top to bottom) as anterior, lateral, posterior, and medial. D) Corresponding session-level estimates at five categorical cortical HVA sampling locations—LM, RL, AL, PM, and AM—in the Allen Brain Observatory Neuropixels dataset. Points, half-violins, bars, and whiskers have the same meanings as in C. The HVA locations are treated categorically rather than positioned or fitted along an anatomical hierarchy axis, providing a matched visual comparison with the categorical V1 locations. E) Stable identity effects and their direct contrast. For each metric, the first two rows show ω²(V1), the bias-corrected fraction of session-level variance explained by V1 location identity, and ω²(HVA), the corresponding fraction explained by HVA identity. The third row shows their difference, Δω² = ω²(HVA) − ω²(V1). Markers and labels show observed estimates, and horizontal whiskers show 95% intervals from bootstrap resampling of whole session blocks, preserving all eligible locations recorded within each sampled session. All three contrast intervals included zero. F–G) Single-neuron response-timescale distributions within V1 and across HVAs, respectively. The same anatomical VISp restriction is applied to MouseV2. Kernel-density ridgelines are weighted so that each session contributes equally and each neuron contributes equally within its session; repeated metric draws from a neuron share that neuron's weight. Vertical lines mark weighted medians, annotations give unique-neuron and session counts, and the displayed range is limited to 0–150 ms. The post-V1 analyses include LM, RL, AL, PM, and AM; LP is excluded.
**Anatomical inclusion for panels C–E:** MouseV2 contributions to the subsequent functional analyses include only units whose own extremum AP channel is labelled VISp. Thus, when a probe's dorsal entry proxy lies outside the independent VISp surface polygon, only its deeper VISp-localized units are used; cells assigned to non-VISp channels are excluded.
